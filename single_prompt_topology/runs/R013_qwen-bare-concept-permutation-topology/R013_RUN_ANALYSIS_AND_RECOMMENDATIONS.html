R013 answers the semantic question more cleanly than R012 because the same three concepts were measured in all six orders without the synthesis instruction or assistant-generation prefix.

# R013 Semantic Synthesis

## 1. Central result

Across all six permutations, the model repeatedly reconstructs the same broad semantic architecture even though the exact tokens, graph edges and community boundaries change substantially with concept order.

The most stable higher-order structure is:

```text
POSITIVE-ALIGNED STRUCTURE

SENSORY / MATERIAL / TECHNICAL
              ↕
PLACE / ENVIRONMENT
              ↕
STRUCTURAL / MEASUREMENT


NEGATIVE-ALIGNED STRUCTURE

SOCIAL / ACTIVITY / INSTITUTIONAL
              ↕
TRANSFORMATION / INTERACTION
              ↕
STRUCTURAL / CATEGORICAL
```

The central finding is therefore not that the model discovers one named topic.

It is that the three concepts repeatedly decompose into a small set of higher-order semantic parents and relations that survive concept reordering much better than their exact lexical and network realisation.

A compact description is:

> A situated sensory-material field organised spatially and structurally, accompanied by a social/activity field whose organisation is relational and dynamic.

The positive side is dominated by a persistent relationship between:

```text
SENSORY / MATERIAL

```

This relation appears throughout the run in many different local forms.

Examples include communities combining:

```text
sensor + laser + apartment + granite + cracks

laser + kitchen + distributors + batteries

sensors + brown + glass + shopping / city material

electrodes + laser + technical objects + place names
```

The meaning is not merely colour or electronics.

The model repeatedly constructs something closer to:

> sensory and technical properties situated within a physical or urban environment.

Structural / measurement material frequently attaches to this pair:

```text
SENSORY / MATERIAL
        ↕
PLACE / ENVIRONMENT
        ↕
STRUCTURAL / MEASUREMENT
```

This is the strongest candidate for the positive-side semantic invariant.

> a social/activity field organised through interactions, transformations and categorical relations.

This is especially important because it explains why Tokyo nightlife does not reduce to a place parent.

Its social component forms a distinct relational structure.

Negative cosine remains geometric anti-alignment with the anchor state, not suppression.

# 8. How the semantic structure develops through the transformer

The six trajectories support the following developmental sequence.

## S01-S03 — broad context acquisition

The anchor begins incorporating all three concepts.

The representation activates:

```text
sensory / technical
place
social activity
structural relations
transformation
representation
```

The parent inventory is broad and weakly differentiated.

Order already matters, but most semantic families are still simultaneously active.

---

## S04-S09 — decomposition and maximum order divergence

The original phrases stop behaving like intact units.

Their components separate.

```text
electro pink
→ sensory / material / technical

curvature density
→ structural / measurement

Tokyo nightlife
→ place
  +
  social / activity
```

Cross-permutation similarity falls sharply.

State 09 is the strongest divergence point.

This is not semantic collapse.

It is the point at which different concept orders produce the most different local organisations of broadly similar semantic material.

---

## S10-S18 — sector differentiation

The semantic system becomes more clearly divided.

Positive topology increasingly favours:

```text
sensory / material
+
place / environment
```

Negative topology increasingly favours:

```text
social / activity
+
transformation / interaction
+
structural / categorical
```

Exact token and community topology remains strongly order-sensitive, but the higher-order parent inventory is becoming clearer.

---

## S19-S23 — semantic compression

The positive side simplifies around sensory/place organisation.

The negative side retains social/relational organisation.

Exact network overlap remains modest, but the number of defensible parent families required to explain the communities decreases.

The representation is becoming easier to describe semantically even while exact topology remains plastic.

---

## S24-S27 — late reintegration

Transformation, representation and structural organisation re-enter the positive topology more clearly.

The broad parent architecture converges across permutations.

The final transformer block therefore contains a more integrated system than the mid-transformer states.

S27 is the correct endpoint for transformer-computed semantic organisation.

---

## S28 — output-facing recoding

S28 is not another transformer layer.

It is post-normalisation.

It performs a very large vocabulary-space recoding while simultaneously making the six permutations more similar again.

Its positive topology becomes strongly representation/operation-oriented.

Its negative topology becomes strongly interaction/distribution-oriented.

S28 is therefore useful as an output-facing representation, but it should not be read as another reasoning step.

# 9. Order changes emphasis more than architecture

The six permutations produce very different exact token networks.

But they do not usually produce entirely different semantic worlds.

Order changes:

```text
which parent dominates
which communities combine
which structures remain autonomous
which relations become explicit
which parent absorbs a mixed community
how strongly each concept-derived branch is expressed
```

What order does not usually change is the broad inventory of higher-order parents.

The dominant result is therefore:

```text
ORDER
    ↓
changes semantic emphasis
and compression

more than

ORDER
    ↓
changes the fundamental
parent architecture
```

This is the key semantic interpretation of the permutation experiment.

---

# 10. Final position is the strongest positional influence

Pairs sharing the same final concept are consistently more similar than pairs sharing the first or middle concept.

This holds through the entire post-context trajectory.

The final concept therefore strongly biases:

```text
token selection
edge structure
community organisation
parent prominence
```

But it does not erase the invariant parent architecture.

This makes the surviving semantic structure more informative.

It survives despite a strong positional bias.

The appropriate interpretation is:

> concept order changes the route and emphasis of semantic organisation, while a smaller higher-order architecture remains recoverable beneath those changes.


The strongest candidate for semantic invariance is therefore not the exact neighbourhood.

# 15. Project-level implication

R010 suggested that semantic communities could be compressed into higher-order parent structures.

R012 suggested that those parents survived one order perturbation better than exact tokens and communities.

R013 provides the stronger test.

All six possible orderings produce large changes in exact topology, yet the same broad semantic parents and relations remain recoverable.

The project progression is therefore:

```text
R010
WHAT STRUCTURES FORM?
        ↓
R012
WHICH STRUCTURES SURVIVE
A LIMITED PERTURBATION?
        ↓
R013
WHICH STRUCTURES SURVIVE
THE COMPLETE ORDER PERMUTATION?
```

The answer from R013 is:

> The exact network is strongly order-conditioned, but a smaller higher-order semantic architecture survives beneath it.

# 16. Central conclusion

The semantic result of R013 is:

> **Electro pink, curvature density and Tokyo nightlife do not converge on a single topic. They decompose into a sensory-material field, a spatial/environmental field, a structural/measurement field and a social/activity field. The positive topology is held together mainly by sensory-material and place relations; the negative topology is held together mainly by social/activity and transformation/interaction relations. Concept order strongly changes the local network and community organisation, but this higher-order parent architecture remains recoverable across all six permutations.**

The most important project-level inference is therefore:

> **Semantic invariance appears at a higher level of abstraction than token or community invariance. The model's persistent representation of the conceptual intersection is better described by relations between higher-order semantic parents than by a fixed vocabulary neighbourhood.**
