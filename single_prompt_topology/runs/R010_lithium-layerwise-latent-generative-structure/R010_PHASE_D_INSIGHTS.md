# R010 Phase D — insights

## Status

**CANONICAL PHASE-D INSIGHTS REPORT**

Phase D analyses curvature, coherence difficulty and the handling of conflicting semantic information across transformer depth.

## Core result

The strongest evidence does not support a simple model in which conflicting information is resolved by immediately suppressing one alternative.

Instead, the transformer trajectory repeatedly follows:

~~~text
preserve competing structures
        ↓
attempt a structural organisation
        ↓
pass through ambiguity when necessary
        ↓
re-partition / re-differentiate
        ↓
select, absorb or assign different roles
        ↓
integrate compatible functions
        ↓
sequence complementary functions
        ↓
isolate a functional core
~~~

The central Phase-D conclusion is:

> **Conflict is often resolved by changing the relationship between surviving structures rather than deleting one of them.**

## Curvature is multidimensional

Phase D separates:

- **geometric curvature** — neighbourhood membership and edge change;
- **partition curvature** — community-assignment and community-count change;
- **semantic curvature** — change in inferred higher-order parent organisation;
- **coherence difficulty** — state-local resistance to confident semantic compression.

A single curvature-density score was tested but is not adequate as the primary explanatory variable because it hides important cases where semantic organisation changes over stable geometry.

## Where coherence was hardest

The highest combined coherence-difficulty states were:

1. **S09 — 0.740**
2. **S10 — 0.685**
3. **S13 — 0.672**
4. **S26 — 0.667**
5. **S27 — 0.607**

The strongest side-specific maxima were:

- **positive:** S13 = **0.969**
- **negative:** S10 = **0.885**

Negative-side difficulty is concentrated earlier. Positive-side difficulty repeatedly reappears into the late transformer.

## Conflict predicts repartitioning

Across internal transitions, the strongest measured association is:

- source coherence difficulty → next partition curvature: **ρ = 0.502**

This means difficult-to-compress states tend to be followed by community reorganisation.

By contrast, curvature does not reliably predict immediate coherence improvement.

Structural change can therefore:

- create conflict;
- expose conflict;
- repartition conflict;
- resolve conflict.

## Negative-side conflict resolves earlier

Negative difficulty remains high through S07–S10 and peaks at S10.

Across S09→S11, previously distinct functions:

- characterisation;
- evaluation;
- prohibition/regulation;
- adverse or controlled change;

stabilise into the persistent T-C control architecture.

Negative difficulty then falls sharply from **0.885 at S10 to 0.219 at S11**.

The conflict is resolved by integration into a broader control relation rather than by eliminating one of the functions.

## Compression can make conflict worse

At S14 the positive side retains separate inquiry/professional and biomedical/physical structures.

S14→S15 compresses them into a broader inquiry/analytical structure, but S15 leaves approximately **44.7%** of semantic-eligible positive material mixed-unresolved.

S15→S16 then produces the highest positive partition curvature in the run (**0.919**), restores a separate health/biomedical structure, and reduces positive coherence difficulty.

This establishes an important mechanism:

~~~text
compression
   ↓
unresolved heterogeneity
   ↓
re-partition
   ↓
restore distinction
~~~

The transformer trajectory is therefore not monotonically compressive.

## S21→S23 — clearest abstraction-conflict episode

At S21→S22:

- positive geometric curvature = **0.054**
- positive semantic curvature = **0.731**
- positive coherence difficulty rises from **0.401 to 0.745**

The underlying neighbourhood barely changes, but the higher-order interpretation does.

Then S22→S23:

- positive partition curvature = **0.831**
- positive semantic curvature = **0.796**
- positive difficulty falls from **0.745 to 0.432**

This is the clearest observed signature of:

~~~text
stable semantic substrate
        ↓
broader abstraction attempted
        ↓
coherence becomes harder
        ↓
community repartition
        ↓
distinction restored
        ↓
coherence improves
~~~

## Integration does not require total assimilation

S26→S27 has the highest positive semantic-curvature score (**0.898**) while positive geometric curvature is only **0.204**.

Applied inquiry and information-production structures integrate into one parent covering **72.1%** of semantic-eligible positive material.

Yet substantial mixed material remains.

The next transition, S27→S28, produces a much larger coherence improvement by restoring a separate health/wellbeing branch.

This suggests:

> **The model can construct a coherent core while leaving heterogeneous satellites outside it.**

## Final state: functional sufficiency, not maximum global coherence

S31 separates:

- analytical/evidential activity;
- professional/information-production activity.

S32 converts these thematic structures into a sequential workflow:

~~~text
data / testing / measurement
        ↓
acquisition / transfer / state change
        ↓
reporting / recording / publishing
~~~

But S32 is not the point of maximum global semantic coherence.

Positive coherence difficulty rises slightly from **0.547 to 0.604**, and the final workflow parent covers only **51.3%** of semantic-eligible positive material.

The terminal state therefore appears to require a sufficiently coherent **functional pathway**, not total semantic unification.

## Positive versus negative conflict handling

~~~text
NEGATIVE SIDE
stabilise boundary / control roles relatively early

POSITIVE SIDE
continue reorganising alternative generative structures
until functional roles become clear
~~~

The negative side is more reactive to difficult states.

The positive side remains more plastic and repeatedly tests alternative abstractions much later.

## Inferred conflict-resolution operations

The current capture supports seven recurring descriptive operations:

1. **Preserve** competing structures rather than immediately erase them.
2. **Partition** incompatible structures when one parent is too broad.
3. **Compress** related structures under a discriminative higher-order relation.
4. **Re-differentiate** when compression leaves unresolved heterogeneity.
5. **Reassign function** while retaining the underlying semantic material.
6. **Integrate only the necessary core**, leaving residual material peripheral.
7. **Sequence complementary functions** into a workflow when they remain distinct but mutually necessary.

## Strongest Phase-D conclusion

~~~text
CONFLICT
= multiple plausible organisations of represented information

        ↓

TRANSFORMER RESPONSE

preserve alternatives
→ test broader organisation
→ repartition if necessary
→ assign distinct roles
→ integrate compatible roles
→ sequence functions
→ isolate a functional core
~~~

The best current interpretation is:

> **The transformer handles conflicting information by discovering a higher-order organisation in which useful competing structures can coexist. Material that cannot be integrated need not be erased; it can remain peripheral while the model constructs a functionally sufficient representation for output.**

## Phase-D boundary

These findings are descriptive reconstructions from R008–R010.

They do not establish a literal symbolic conflict-resolution algorithm or expose a natural-language inner monologue.

They describe how the measured and inferred representation changes across transformer depth.
