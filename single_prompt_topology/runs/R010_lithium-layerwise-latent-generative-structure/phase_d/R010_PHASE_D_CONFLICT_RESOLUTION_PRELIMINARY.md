# R010 Phase D — preliminary conflict-resolution findings

## Status

**PRELIMINARY CONFLICT-RESOLUTION ANALYSIS**

This report is subordinate to the frozen Phase-D measurement protocol and the conflict-resolution addendum.

It asks how conflicting semantic information is handled across transformer depth. It does not claim access to literal internal thoughts or a known causal algorithm. The findings describe the evolution of the measured neighbourhoods and the inferred R010 higher-order structures.

## Executive finding

The transformer does not appear to resolve conflict primarily by immediately eliminating one interpretation.

Across the run, the recurring pattern is:

```text
COMPETING / HETEROGENEOUS INFORMATION
        ↓
PRESERVE SEPARATELY OR LEAVE UNRESOLVED
        ↓
RE-PARTITION / REASSIGN FUNCTIONAL ROLE
        ↓
SELECT, ABSORB OR INTEGRATE
        ↓
CONSTRUCT A HIGHER-ORDER RELATION
        ↓
RETAIN NON-ESSENTIAL MATERIAL AS SATELLITE / RESIDUAL
```

The strongest result is therefore:

> **Conflict resolution is usually achieved by structural reorganisation, not simple deletion.**

Several mechanisms recur:

1. **Selection** — one interpretation loses independent parent status.
2. **Partition** — conflicting material is separated into distinct parents.
3. **Absorption** — a previously independent structure becomes a satellite/component.
4. **Integration** — separate structures become children of a broader parent.
5. **Role differentiation** — semantically related material is retained under different functions.
6. **Constraint separation** — negative-side control/boundary structures remain distinct from positive-side generative structures.
7. **Terminal functionalisation** — thematic structures are recoded as sequential roles in a workflow.

## 1. Early negative-side conflict consolidates into a control architecture

The clearest early conflict-resolution episode occurs around S08–S11.

Phase B reconstructs initially separate negative strands:

- scientific / clinical / technical assessment;
- regulation / prohibition;
- adverse evaluation;
- destructive or controlled change.

At S09 these remain distinct. The negative side has a high mixed-unresolved semantic load of approximately **29.8%**.

At S10 the previously separated assessment and adverse/change strands merge into the T-C parent:

```text
CHARACTERISATION
      +
EVALUATION
      +
CONSTRAINT / PROHIBITION
      +
CONTROLLED / DESTRUCTIVE CHANGE
```

The negative mixed-unresolved load falls to approximately **12.6%** at S10 and to **0%** at S11, while the dominant negative parent reaches **57.4%** semantic-eligible coverage at S11.

The important point is that the apparent conflict is not resolved by choosing:

```text
assessment OR adverse change
```

Instead the model finds a broader relation under which both are functional components:

```text
assess state
      ↓
judge / constrain state
      ↓
permit, prohibit or change state
```

**Resolution mechanism: integration into a control relation.**

## 2. S14–S17 shows conflict created by over-compression and resolved by re-differentiation

This sequence is especially informative.

At S14 the positive side retains two broad parents:

- coordinated inquiry / professional action;
- biomedical / interacting physical-system organisation.

At S15 they compress to one dominant inquiry/analytical parent covering **55.3%** of semantic-eligible positive material.

But this compression leaves a very large unresolved remainder:

- positive mixed-unresolved load at S15: approximately **44.7%**.

The unresolved communities contain:

- biomedical/respiratory + affective/bodily actions;
- food/business/shopping/healthcare/housing/platform material.

At S16 the representation re-differentiates:

```text
PROCESS / TRANSFORMATION
        +
HEALTH / BIOMEDICAL
```

The mixed-unresolved load falls sharply to approximately **8.1%**.

Phase C independently finds strong positive community repartitioning at S15→S16, with positive ARI **0.296**, the second-lowest positive ARI in the run after S00→S01.

At S17 the health parent again loses independent status and is absorbed into a broader developmental/transformational process architecture.

This sequence suggests:

```text
separate structures
      ↓
compression
      ↓
too much unresolved heterogeneity remains
      ↓
re-partition
      ↓
roles become clearer
      ↓
later absorption under broader process relation
```

**Resolution mechanism: temporary re-differentiation followed by absorption.**

This is evidence against a purely monotonic “compress everything” model. Compression can create unresolved conflict, after which later layers restore distinctions.

## 3. S21–S24 contains abstraction conflict over a stable substrate

This region is the strongest candidate for conflict between higher-order interpretations rather than conflict between token populations.

Phase C shows extremely stable geometry at S21→S22:

- positive membership Jaccard **0.894**;
- negative membership Jaccard **0.900**;
- positive edge Jaccard **0.761**;
- negative edge Jaccard **0.773**.

Yet Phase B reconstructs repeated changes in positive higher-order organisation:

```text
S21
development / measurement
+
mediated professional/interface activity

        ↓

S22
one analytical inquiry /
research-enterprise parent

        ↓

S23
health / wellbeing reappears as a separate parent

        ↓

S24
health loses parent status
+
mediated professional branch reappears
```

State-local ambiguity behaves consistently with this interpretation:

- S21 positive residual semantic load: approximately **30.5%**;
- S22 positive mixed load: **16.4%**, residual load **43.8%**;
- S23 has no mixed-unresolved positive community but four irreducible semantic communities;
- S24 positive mixed load rises to approximately **24.5%**.

The underlying token/edge substrate is largely stable while different higher-order explanations compete.

**Resolution mechanism: repeated role reassignment and alternative abstraction.**

This region appears to show the transformer testing whether the same material is best represented as:

- development/inquiry;
- professional information activity;
- health/wellbeing;
- or combinations of these.

The conflict is therefore primarily **relational**, not lexical.

## 4. S26→S27 resolves a core operational conflict by higher-order integration

At S26 the positive side separates:

- applied inquiry / measurement / developmental change;
- digital interface / information-production systems.

The first parent covers **49.7%** and the second **22.2%** of semantic-eligible positive material.

At S27 they merge into one broad parent:

> analytical, communicative and professional information activity

covering **72.1%** of semantic-eligible positive material.

Phase C shows that this occurs over stable geometry:

- positive membership Jaccard **0.866**;
- positive edge Jaccard **0.709**.

This means conflict between:

```text
ANALYSE / MEASURE
        vs
COMMUNICATE / PRODUCE INFORMATION
```

is resolved not by selecting one side but by recognising them as parts of one organised information-producing activity.

Importantly, approximately **27.9%** of positive semantic-eligible material at S27 remains mixed-unresolved.

The transformer therefore does not need to assimilate all semantic material to resolve the core conflict.

**Resolution mechanism: higher-order integration while leaving heterogeneous satellites outside the core.**

## 5. S29–S32 shows final conflict resolution by functional sequencing

This is the strongest late-stage result.

At S29 and S30 the positive side has one broad dominant parent, but substantial semantic material remains internally mixed:

- S29 positive mixed load: approximately **37.2%**;
- S30 positive mixed load: approximately **29.7%**.

At S31 the broad structure splits into two functionally distinct parents:

1. analytical interpretation / evidential evaluation;
2. mediated professional / enterprise / information-production activity.

The two-parent reconstruction covers far more of the semantic material than a single narrow workflow would, while positive mixed load falls to approximately **16.7%**.

Phase A records a serious competing alternative at S31: merge the two positive parents into one broad analytical/professional information-activity parent.

The preferred reconstruction instead retains the distinction.

At S32 the two branches are not merely merged thematically. They are recoded into a sequential functional workflow:

```text
DATA / TESTING / MEASUREMENT
        ↓
ACQUISITION / TRANSFER / STATE CHANGE
        ↓
REPORTING / RECORDING / PUBLISHING
```

This is qualitatively different from saying the branches are “similar”.

Their apparent conflict is resolved by assigning them **different positions in one procedure**.

The final workflow parent covers **51.3%** of semantic-eligible positive material. Approximately **48.7%** remains mixed or irreducible.

This is important:

> **The terminal representation does not maximise semantic coverage. It extracts a sharper functional core and leaves non-essential heterogeneous material peripheral.**

The final resolution therefore looks like:

```text
COMPETING THEMATIC ORGANISATIONS
        ↓
FUNCTIONAL DIFFERENTIATION
        ↓
SEQUENTIAL COMPOSITION
        ↓
WORKFLOW
```

**Resolution mechanism: terminal functionalisation / sequencing.**

## 6. Positive and negative sides solve different conflict problems

The negative side stabilises earlier.

From roughly S10 onward it repeatedly reconstructs:

```text
T-C
characterisation
+ evaluation
+ constraint
+ controlled/adverse change

alongside

T-A
authority
+ territory
+ institutional/social order
```

Later negative-side conflict is therefore often handled by **compartmentalisation**:

- control/change remains one function;
- authority/order remains another;
- pathology and material environments remain satellites or secondary contexts.

The positive side remains much more plastic.

It repeatedly asks how domains and operations should be grouped into:

- inquiry;
- development;
- health;
- professional activity;
- information production;
- finally a workflow.

A useful asymmetry therefore emerges:

```text
NEGATIVE SIDE
resolve conflict early by stabilising boundary/control roles

POSITIVE SIDE
continue reorganising competing generative roles until late layers
```

## 7. Provisional conflict-resolution methodology

Across the transformer, the data currently support the following sequence:

```text
1. PRESERVE
Do not immediately erase competing semantic structures.

2. PARTITION
Keep incompatible structures separate when one parent would be too broad.

3. TEST COMPRESSION
Attempt broader parents when relations support them.

4. RE-DIFFERENTIATE IF NECESSARY
If compression leaves too much heterogeneous material, restore distinctions.

5. ASSIGN ROLES
Convert domain differences into functional differences.

6. INTEGRATE COMPLEMENTARY ROLES
Place compatible functions under a higher-order relation.

7. SEQUENCE
When functions remain distinct but complementary, organise them into a workflow.

8. LEAVE RESIDUAL MATERIAL OUTSIDE THE CORE
Coherence does not require every semantic community to be absorbed.
```

## Strongest provisional conclusion

The transformer appears to address conflicting information by **preserving alternatives long enough to determine whether they should be separated, subordinated, integrated or assigned different functional roles**.

The final resolution is often not:

```text
A wins
B disappears
```

but:

```text
A and B are both retained
        ↓
their relationship is redefined
        ↓
a higher-order structure explains why both are present
```

At the terminal state, this becomes especially explicit: analytical/evidential and information-production structures are resolved by turning them into different stages of a functional workflow.

## Boundary

These findings are preliminary. The frozen Phase-D curvature and coherence rankings still need to be calculated independently and then compared against these conflict episodes.

No claim is made that the model explicitly executes the named resolution mechanisms as symbolic operations. They are descriptive reconstructions of the observed layer-wise structural changes.
