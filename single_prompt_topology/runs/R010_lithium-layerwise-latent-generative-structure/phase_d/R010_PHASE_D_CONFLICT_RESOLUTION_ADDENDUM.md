# R010 Phase D — conflict-resolution analysis addendum

## Status

**ADDED AFTER PRIMARY PHASE-D PROTOCOL FREEZE**

This addendum does not alter the frozen curvature/coherence metrics. It adds a second analytical objective prompted by the emerging research question:

> **How does the transformer handle conflicting information while constructing the representation used for next-token prediction?**

The primary Phase-D protocol remains unchanged. Conflict-resolution analysis is performed alongside it so that ambiguity, curvature and conflict are not conflated.

## Conflict definition

A conflict exists when two or more simultaneously represented structures support materially different interpretations, roles, classifications or transformations of the same developing representation.

Conflict is not defined merely as:

- positive versus negative score;
- multiple communities;
- multiple retained parents;
- lexical noise;
- ordinary semantic diversity.

The analysis requires evidence that competing structures cannot all serve the same higher-order role without qualification, partition, suppression, reframing or integration.

## Conflict classes

### C1 — intra-community conflict

A single measured community contains two or more incompatible organising principles.

Primary evidence:

- Phase-A disposition = `mixed-unresolved`;
- R009/R010 descriptions explicitly state that distinct semantic organisations coexist.

Question:

> Does the next layer split, suppress, absorb or reinterpret the mixed community?

### C2 — competing-parent conflict

Two or more plausible parent reconstructions compete for the same communities or semantic material.

Primary evidence:

- serious Phase-A alternative reconstruction;
- Moderate or lower parent confidence caused by competing grouping hypotheses;
- one community has meaningful secondary links to multiple candidate parents.

Question:

> Which parent relation survives, and what evidence changes its status?

### C3 — role conflict

The same semantic domain persists but changes functional role.

Examples may include:

- health as independent parent versus satellite;
- professional/interface material as independent branch versus component of a broader workflow;
- material environments attaching alternately to authority and control structures.

Question:

> Is the conflict resolved by selecting one role, allowing role coexistence, or constructing a higher-order relation that contains both?

### C4 — positive/negative interpretive conflict

Related semantic material appears on both positive and negative sides but supports different functional interpretations.

This is not automatically contradiction or migration.

Question:

> Does the transformer differentiate the same domain into generative versus constraining/adverse roles?

### C5 — abstraction conflict

The underlying token neighbourhood remains stable while the preferred higher-order interpretation changes.

Primary evidence:

- high membership/edge Jaccard;
- substantial Phase-B semantic merge/split/reframing.

Question:

> How can the same substrate support multiple abstractions, and what relation makes one abstraction more coherent at the next state?

## Resolution mechanisms

Each conflict episode should be classified by the mechanism that resolves or stabilises it:

1. **Selection** — one competing structure loses parent status or alignment.
2. **Partition** — ambiguous material separates into distinct communities or parents.
3. **Absorption** — one structure becomes a satellite/component of another.
4. **Integration** — previously separate structures become children of a broader parent.
5. **Role differentiation** — the same material is retained but assigned different functions.
6. **Constraint separation** — generative and boundary/control roles become distinct.
7. **Persistence without resolution** — conflict remains across subsequent states.
8. **Terminal recoding** — a late representational reset converts thematic structures into a functional workflow.

## Primary conflict questions

For every detected conflict episode:

1. What information is in conflict?
2. At what structural level does the conflict occur?
   - token/community;
   - parent;
   - functional role;
   - positive/negative side;
   - higher-order abstraction.
3. Does the transformer preserve both alternatives or eliminate one?
4. If both survive, are they:
   - partitioned;
   - assigned different roles;
   - integrated under a broader structure?
5. What changes in the next state?
6. Does resolution require geometric change, community repartitioning, semantic reinterpretation, or a combination?
7. Does the resolved structure later reappear as a new conflict?

## Candidate regions from existing evidence

These are hypotheses only and must be tested against the full trajectory:

- **S08–S11:** assessment versus adverse/change strands consolidate into the negative T-C control architecture.
- **S14–S17:** process versus health/domain organisation repeatedly merges and re-splits.
- **S21–S24:** analytical/professional/health structures repeatedly change higher-order grouping despite stable geometry.
- **S26–S27:** applied inquiry and information-production structures integrate under one broad positive parent.
- **S30–S32:** analytical/evidential and professional/information-production branches split, then terminally recombine into a functional workflow.

## Output requirement

The Phase-D conflict report must distinguish:

```text
CONFLICT PRESENT
        ↓
WHAT COMPETES?
        ↓
HOW IS THE CONFLICT REPRESENTED?
        ↓
WHAT CHANGES?
        ↓
RESOLUTION MECHANISM
        ↓
SURVIVING / INTEGRATED STRUCTURE
```

The principal result should therefore not merely identify where coherence is low.

It should identify **how the transformer converts competing semantic structures into a representation sufficiently coherent to support the next-token distribution**.
