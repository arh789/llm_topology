# Analysis Brief

## Purpose

Analyse the proposed LLM semantic-topology research design without treating its
central hypothesis as established. Use `PROJECT_HANDOVER.md` as the governing
design and `ZOMBIE_CASE_STUDY.md` as a motivating behavioural case.

## Available evidence

- A project handover defining the intended constructs, phases and success
  criteria.
- A structured interpretation of one published, human-supervised ChatGPT
  interaction.

There are currently no experimental data, activation captures, statistical
results or causal interventions in this package.

## Requested analysis

1. Test whether stable coherence, unstable coherence, Addition A and Addition B
   are operationally distinguishable before any model is run.
2. Identify the smallest controlled stimulus set that can falsify, not merely
   illustrate, the proposed relationship.
3. Design the minimum viable Pythia experiment, including exact conditions,
   controls, repeated trials and recorded metadata.
4. Specify a behavioural annotation schema that separates recognition,
   preservation, resolution, omission and concealment of contradictions.
5. Recommend the first hidden-state representation and distance analyses while
   explaining why each measurement addresses the research question.
6. Identify linguistic, positional, prompt-length, sampling, context-history
   and researcher-selection confounds.
7. State what result would count as support, a null result or evidence against
   the hypothesis.
8. Separate later geometric and causal work from what the first experiment can
   honestly establish.

## Required epistemic boundaries

- Do not assume semantic conflict equals high curvature.
- Do not treat generated explanations as measurements of internal computation.
- Do not treat textual consistency as factual correctness.
- Do not describe a geometric property without specifying its representation,
  layer, token or pooling rule, metric, neighbourhood and estimator.
- Do not compare raw coordinates across unrelated model architectures.
- Keep behavioural, geometric and causal conclusions separate.
- Treat null and contradictory results as valid outcomes.

## Preferred first output

Produce a concise experimental specification containing:

- the primary falsifiable hypothesis;
- competing explanations;
- a small table of stimuli and controls;
- precise A and B intervention rules;
- behavioural labels and adjudication rules;
- activation capture locations;
- initial metrics;
- replication and uncertainty requirements;
- stop conditions before scaling to larger models.

Where the supplied material is insufficient, name the unresolved decision and
explain how different choices would change the interpretation. Do not silently
fill gaps with assumptions.
